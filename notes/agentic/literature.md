# Agentic delegation: what the literature offers

Literature search of 2026-10-19 for the question: can the delegation of a global PolySTL contract to port-local
component contracts be computed by **agents** (LLM proposers or symbolic search) that are constrained by the
contract calculus, with an **SMT verifier** that checks each proposal and returns counterexamples?

**How the list was built.** About 30 arXiv queries were run through the arxiv MCP server (`search_papers`), with
several phrasings for each of the six topics. Every paper listed below had its abstract retrieved with
`get_abstract` (50 papers). The summaries come from those abstracts only. No full text was read, so details
beyond the abstract are marked as unverified where they matter. BibTeX for all 50 entries is in
`notes/agentic/literature.bib` (exported with `export_citations`).

**Not repeated here.** Works already surveyed in `lit/existing-work.md` are cited by their key in
`lit/existing-work.bib` and are not listed again: Pacti, Incer's quotient, Benveniste et al.,
Albarghouthi–Dillig–Gurfinkel multi-abduction, ∀∃ Horn clauses (Beyene et al.), Finkbeiner–Passing certifying
synthesis, Finkbeiner–Geier–Passing, Pnueli–Rosner, Finkbeiner–Schewe, Filippidis–Murray, Kanno et al.,
Marchesini et al., Charitidou–Dimarogonas, Anand–Nayak–Schmuck, Iannopollo et al., Dardik–Kang.
Classic results that are not on arXiv, such as the L\* assumption learning of Cobleigh–Giannakopoulou–Păsăreanu
(2003), were not verifiable with this tool and are left out.

**Notation from `docs/complete.tex`.** (V1) is `A_P ∧ ⋀_i S_i ⊨ G_P`, (V2) is `A_P ∧ ⋀_i S_i ⊨ A_j`,
(R) is receptiveness and (K) is causality. The seed is the strategy graph `y = s_y`, and the sweep is the
quotient-and-projection enlargement. Text marked **[analysis]** is this survey's own reading and is not a claim
made by any cited paper.

## (a) Verified papers by topic

### Topic 1: Synthesis of assume–guarantee contracts and components; CEGIS foundations

| arXiv | Title | Authors | Year | Verdict for our question |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1602.00148 | Towards Synthesis from AG Contracts involving Infinite Theories | Katis, Whalen, Gacek | 2016 | ∀∃ realizability over LRA, plus Skolem extraction with AE-VAL. Gives a checker for (R)/(K). One component only. |
| 1610.05867 | Synthesis from AG Contracts using Skolemized Proofs of Realizability | Katis, Fedyukovich, Gacek, et al. | 2016 | Same line of work, mature (JSyn). Implements Skolem functions from ∀∃ proofs, i.e. our "witness" step. |
| 1709.04986 | Validity-Guided Synthesis of Reactive Systems from AG Contracts | Katis, Fedyukovich, Guo, et al. | 2017 | An IC3-style ∀∃ fixpoint for safety contracts over theories. A symbolic alternative to full QE for a single component. |
| 1411.4604 | AG Synthesis for Concurrent Reactive Programs with Partial Information | Bloem, Chatterjee, Jacobs, Könighofer | 2014 | AG synthesis with hidden local variables. Undecidable in many cases, so they use bounded synthesis. This is the partial-information limit for agents. |
| 1810.10636 | Compositional Set Invariance in Network Systems with AG Contracts | Chen, Anderson, Kalsi, Low, Ames | 2018 | pSTL templates as contracts, found by value iteration or an epigraph method. Needs dynamics. Template idea only. |
| 2311.03164 | A Contract Negotiation Scheme for Safety Verification of Interconnected Systems | Tan, Papachristodoulou, Dimarogonas | 2023 | Iterative negotiation of local contracts (SOS barrier functions). A negotiation loop, but model-based. |
| 2412.13114 | Contract-based Design and Verification of MAS with Quantitative Temporal Requirements | Dewes, Dimitrova | 2024 | Quantitative AG contracts for local tasks plus a shared constraint in LTL[F]. The local specs are given. Peripheral. |
| 1809.02283 | Relational Program Synthesis | Wang, Wang, Dillig | 2018 | CEGIS for **several programs that jointly satisfy one relational spec**. The closest CEGIS shape to n-component delegation. |
| 1505.03953 | A Theory of Formal Synthesis via Inductive Learning | Jha, Seshia | 2015 | OGIS/CEGIS theory: the power of different counterexample types, and convergence over finite spaces. Frames the agent loop. |
| 1407.5397 | Are There Good Mistakes? A Theoretical Analysis of CEGIS | Jha, Seshia | 2014 | CEGIS termination over infinite spaces is not guaranteed. Minimal counterexamples add no power. A limit on the loop. |
| 1502.04464 | Counterexample Guided Quantifier Instantiation for Synthesis in CVC4 | Reynolds, Deters, Kuncak, Tinelli, Barrett | 2015 | Synthesis of ∃∀ functions inside an SMT solver, with single-invocation specs. A ready symbolic proposer for strategies. |
| 2604.15533 | Verification Modulo Tested Library Contracts | Uppar, Muhammad, Prabhu, D'Souza, Madhusudan, Murali | 2026 | Counterexample-guided learning of **several modular contracts** that are adequate for a client, with ICE-based CHC solvers. The closest multi-unknown contract CEGIS, but it has a client program. |

- **1602.00148.** Builds on an earlier realizability check for AG contracts over infinite theories (LIA/LRA, UF). The check is k-induction-like and uses ∀∃ quantification. The paper turns it into synthesis by using AE-VAL, a Skolemizing solver for ∀∃ formulas, to extract implementations, and discusses robustness challenges. *Verdict:* this is the existing "is this component contract receptive/causal, and give me a strategy" check. It is directly usable as the (R)/(K) verifier, but it synthesizes one component from its own contract, not a decomposition.
- **1610.05867.** JSyn proves realizability of safety AG contracts (Lustre) by k-induction over ∀∃ formulas. It then reads Skolem functions off the validity proofs and compiles them to C. *Verdict:* our elimination-with-witnesses step (Lemma 3 of `complete.tex`) does the same job. This shows the step is engineering-proven in LRA.
- **1709.04986.** JSyn-vg reduces synthesis to the validity of a set of ∀∃ formulas and refines the problem space PDR-style by blocking unsafe regions until a fixpoint of safe reactions. A witness is then turned into an implementation. It fixes soundness problems of the k-inductive version. *Verdict:* a scalable symbolic search for per-component ∀∃ obligations, and a candidate replacement for monolithic QE when we check (R)/(K).
- **1411.4604.** Defines assume–guarantee synthesis under partial information: local variables are hidden from other processes. It shows high worst-case complexity and undecidability in many settings, and gives a pragmatic bounded-synthesis algorithm. *Verdict:* hidden variables are exactly what forces our forwarding. The undecidability results bound what any agent can be guaranteed to find.
- **1810.10636.** Robust invariant sets for networked systems, with AG contracts written in parametric STL templates. Known monotone templates are refined by value iteration; otherwise an epigraph method finds a valid contract with linear complexity for sparse networks. *Verdict:* template-parametrized STL contracts are a natural action space for a symbolic proposer, but this method needs dynamics.
- **2311.03164.** Synthesis of control barrier functions for interconnected nonlinear systems. The problem is split into small SOS problems for local contracts, and the subsystems negotiate at the contract level. *Verdict:* precedent for an iterative inter-component negotiation loop. It is model-based and SOS-based, so it is not usable directly.
- **2412.13114.** Proposes quantitative AG contracts for multi-agent systems whose requirements are local LTL[F] tasks plus a shared safety constraint, so that design and verification are compositional. *Verdict:* the contracts capture coordination, but the split into local and shared parts is given. Peripheral.
- **1809.02283.** Relational program synthesis: synthesize one or more programs that *collectively* satisfy a relational spec. It combines CEGIS with relational version-space learning (the Relish tool). *Verdict:* the only CEGIS found whose unknowns are several programs coupled by one spec. This is the structural template for a joint proposer over per-component strategies.
- **1505.03953.** A formal framework for oracle-guided inductive synthesis (OGIS), with CEGIS as one instance. It characterizes how the counterexample type and the learner's memory affect synthesis power, and relates convergence to teaching dimension for finite spaces. *Verdict:* gives the vocabulary and the guarantees, or lack of them, for any LLM-plus-SMT loop we design.
- **1407.5397.** Shows that CEGIS is complete for finite candidate spaces but may not terminate for infinite ones. Minimal counterexamples give the same power as arbitrary ones, and history-bounded counterexamples give incomparable power. *Verdict:* our candidate space (piecewise-affine strategies over ℚ) is infinite, so there is no termination guarantee without a size bound. This matches the semi-decision remark in `complete.tex` §9.
- **1502.04464.** The first synthesis engine inside an SMT solver (CVC4). It extracts solution functions from unsatisfiability proofs of negated synthesis conjectures, uses counterexample-guided quantifier instantiation, and has a dedicated algorithm for single-invocation specs. *Verdict:* a per-component strategy problem with all inputs fixed is single-invocation, so this is a symbolic proposer we could run without an LLM.
- **2604.15533.** Verification modulo tested library contracts. It synthesizes modular or "contextual" contracts for library methods that suffice to prove the client correct and also pass testing. The learner is a counterexample-guided ICE/CHC learner that talks to a constraint solver and a test engine (the DUALIS tool). *Verdict:* the closest existing loop that learns **several** contracts at once from a top-level correctness obligation. It differs from ours because the client program is present and there is no I/O receptiveness or time.

### Topic 2: Learning-based assume–guarantee reasoning and ICE learning

| arXiv | Title | Authors | Year | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1309.5140 | Abstraction and Learning for Infinite-State Compositional Verification | Giannakopoulou, Păsăreanu | 2013 | Extends L\*-style assumption learning to infinite state via abstraction. Needs component models. |
| 2605.15294 | An L# Based Algorithm for Active Learning of Minimal Separating Automata | Laumen, Snel, Vaandrager | 2026 | Current best active learner for separating DFAs (the contextual-assumption problem). Finite alphabets only. |
| 2207.10534 | Assume, Guarantee or Repair | Frenkel, Grumberg, Păsăreanu, Sheinvald | 2022 | Learning-based AG that verifies *or repairs*, with first-order constraints via abstraction. Needs programs. |
| 1712.09418 | Horn-ICE Learning for Synthesizing Invariants and Contracts | D'Souza, Ezudheen, Garg, Madhusudan, Neider | 2017 | Learner from Horn **implication counterexamples**. Our (V1) counterexamples have exactly this multi-premise shape. |
| 2205.14943 | Data-driven Numerical Invariant Synthesis with Automatic Generation of Attributes | Bouajjani, Boutglay, Habermehl | 2022 | ICE decision-tree learning over numerical domains, with attributes found from convex separators. A candidate learner for polytopic S_i. |

- **1309.5140.** Learning-based AG verification had been limited to finite-state systems. This paper interleaves abstraction and learning to handle infinite-state components, and discusses interface generation. *Verdict:* the assumption is learned for a *given* component against a *given* property. That is the one-unknown quotient case, so it is relevant only to the sweep step.
- **2605.15294.** An L#-inspired active learner for a minimal DFA separating two disjoint languages, with an explicit application to learning contextual assumptions in compositional verification. It beats prior separating-automata learners. *Verdict:* shows the state of the art in automata-based assumption learning. It does not transfer to real-valued PolySTL.
- **2207.10534.** AGR verifies communicating C-like programs against properties and repairs them when verification fails, using learning-based AG reasoning over finite abstractions with first-order constraints. *Verdict:* precedent for "verify or modify" loops. The repaired object is a program, not a delegated contract.
- **1712.09418.** Extends ICE (implication counterexamples) to Horn-ICE samples, whose implications can have several premises. A polynomial-time decision-tree learner learns invariants and contracts for sequential and concurrent programs. *Verdict:* **[analysis]** a (V1) counterexample is a trace x with `A_P(x)`, every `S_i(x|ports(i))` true, and `G_P(x)` false. The learner must make at least one `S_i` reject its projection. That is a Horn sample with n unknowns in the body and no head, so this is the right learner model for a symbolic or LLM proposer that has to assign blame.
- **2205.14943.** ICE-DT learning of numerical invariants. Its new piece is the automatic discovery of attributes (linear inequalities) from a convex separator of positive and negative states that is consistent with implications. *Verdict:* gives a way to propose the atoms (half-spaces) of polytopic contracts from counterexample data, instead of fixing the normals as Marchesini et al. do.

- **2303.04864.** nl2spec uses LLMs to translate unstructured natural language into LTL. It maps subformulas back to NL fragments so users can edit sub-translations. *Verdict:* specification *authoring*; it says nothing about decomposition.
- **2305.07766.** NL2TL builds a 28K NL–TL dataset with LLM help and fine-tunes T5 on "lifted" formulas whose atomic propositions are hidden, reaching >95% accuracy with little domain data. *Verdict:* authoring only.
- **2605.06483.** ReasonSTL splits NL→STL into reasoning, deterministic tool calls and formula construction. It trains a local 4B model with process rewards and introduces STL-Bench. *Verdict:* evidence that LLMs can reliably emit syntactically valid STL with real-valued predicates, which a proposer would need. It does not decompose.
- **2310.04870.** Lemur formalizes LLM-plus-verifier interaction as transition rules (proposals of invariants and sub-goals that the verifier checks) and proves the calculus sound. *Verdict:* **the model for stating soundness of an agentic delegation loop.** Proposals are untrusted, and every accepted step is discharged by the solver.
- **2310.09342.** LLMs can produce correct loop invariants zero-shot but need many samples, so many verifier calls. A contrastive ranker puts correct invariants first. *Verdict:* a cost-control device for later.
- **2311.07948.** Curates loop-verification problems, prompts LLMs for inductive invariants, checks them with sound tools, and shows the combination improves on symbolic-only verification. *Verdict:* positive evidence that LLM proposals plus sound checking work on single-unknown problems.
- **2309.16436.** LLMs generate plans and Z3 checks them. Counterexamples go back to the LLM in dialog until the plan is correct; the SMT queries are also generated from NL. *Verdict:* the plain CEGIS-with-LLM baseline. It has a single unknown, with no composition.
- **2403.03997.** Shows that GPT-3.5 alone is beaten by enumerative SyGuS solvers. Integrating LLM calls into a weighted probabilistic enumerator, which feeds enumerator progress back to the LLM, beats both and the competition winner. *Verdict:* strong evidence that LLMs should *bias* a symbolic search, not replace it.
- **2606.11521.** Studies LLMs as learners in counterexample-guided regex induction with a verifier teacher. Counterexample clustering, regularization, reflection and repair raise the success rate on the hardest groups (3.2%→38.1% and 38.9%→74.1%). *Verdict:* the most direct empirical evidence on *how* to present counterexamples to an LLM proposer.
- **2509.23061.** DafnyCOMP has 300 synthesized multi-function programs. LLMs that do well on single functions fail sharply on compositional specification generation, with fragile specs and broken cross-function reasoning. *Verdict:* **a warning that applies to us directly.** Choosing an interface between components is where LLMs fail, so the cross-component part must be handled symbolically (forwarding, quotient).
- **2510.12702.** Defines NL2Contract (pre- and postconditions) and finds that LLM-inferred contracts are mostly sound, discriminate bugs, and reduce verifier false alarms compared with postconditions alone. *Verdict:* LLMs can propose *assumptions* as well as guarantees, though here they work from code and comments, not from a global requirement.
- **2610.23771.** HornCraft: an LLM proposes "derivation power under-approximations" for CHCs, with symbolic intermediate-state witnesses, so that SMT validation needs no extra quantifiers. Validated pieces are composed and reused. *Verdict:* **[analysis]** the same trick our setting allows. If the proposal carries its witness (a strategy), every check stays quantifier-free.
- **2511.09363.** An LLM agent framework for barrier certificates: it proposes and refines templates, verifies with SMT, and co-synthesizes controllers. Over 90% of 100 benchmark systems succeed, and the benchmark is released. *Verdict:* the nearest analogue on the CPS side, showing that LLM template discovery plus SMT can work for real-arithmetic certificates.
- **2506.13131.** AlphaEvolve evolves code with an LLM pipeline and automated evaluators and found new provably correct algorithms. *Verdict:* a plausible outer search if strategies are represented as programs and scored by the SMT verifier, but it gives no guarantees.

### Topic 4: Multi-agent and agentic LLM verification frameworks

| arXiv | Title | Authors | Year | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2409.13082 | AutoVerus: Automated Proof Generation for Rust Code | Yang, Li, Misu, et al. | 2024 | A network of LLM agents in phases (generate, refine, debug from verifier errors). An orchestration pattern. |
| 2605.21434 | Agentic Model Checking | Sun, Liu, Kroening, Xue | 2026 | "Agents propose, solvers verify". Specs are inferred **top-down from caller context**, and each function is checked against callee stubs. Closest architecture. |
| 2610.05132 | SpecAgent | Ma, Wang, Liu, Wang, Bu | 2026 | Dependency-aware planning and *revisiting* earlier specs on cascading failure. Analogous to a sweep over components. |
| 2608.30803 | Schwarz: Solver-Aware Agentic Program Verification | Ke, Wu, Li | 2026 | Makes SMT failures local and repairable (obligation-level snapshots, theory-aware policies). A design for feedback granularity. |
| 2609.18128 | Symbolic Temporal Supervision of LLM Agents Using Contracts | Xiao, Nuzzo | 2026 | LTLf AG contracts compiled to DFAs *supervise* LLM agents. Opposite direction; usable for constraining the proposers' tool use. |

- **2409.13082.** AutoVerus orchestrates several LLM agents through three phases (preliminary proof, refinement with generic tips, debugging from verification errors) to produce Verus proofs for Rust. It solves >90% of 150 tasks. *Verdict:* multi-agent orchestration for a single verification target. The agents are roles, not owners of components.
- **2605.21434.** Couples LLM agents with bounded model checking. Agents infer specs in a restricted DSL that translates deterministically to assume/assert, select checks, classify counterexamples and propose refinements; BMC makes every soundness-relevant decision. Specs are inferred top-down from callers, and each function is verified in isolation with callees replaced by postcondition stubs, so refinements propagate to callers. *Verdict:* **the architecture closest to agentic delegation.** It moves a caller's requirement into callee obligations, verifies compositionally, and validates counterexamples. What it lacks: a global contract algebra, real-valued time, and receptiveness.
- **2610.05132.** SpecAgent synthesizes ACSL specifications for real C programs with dependency-aware planning, retrieval, agentic repair that revisits *dependent* specifications when failures cascade, and a critique of specification strength. *Verdict:* revisiting upstream contracts after a downstream failure is the agentic counterpart of a Gauss–Seidel sweep. Its "strength critique" corresponds to our maximality step.
- **2608.30803.** Schwarz turns failed SMT verification into obligation-local repair tasks, using program-point snapshots, local lemmas and theory-aware solver policies. It solves 95.2% and 91.5% of two benchmark sets. *Verdict:* argues that the verifier should return *which* obligation failed and where. For us that means per-component, per-time blame, not a bare "sat".
- **2609.18128.** ContrAgent captures an agent's tool-call traces over checkable predicates and specifies required behaviour as LTLf AG contracts compiled to DFAs, used for online gating and offline grading. *Verdict:* contracts *on* the agents rather than contracts *by* the agents. This could constrain what proposer agents may emit.

### Topic 5: STL mining, synthesis and decomposition (beyond `lit/existing-work.md` §2)

| arXiv | Title | Authors | Year | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1603.00814 | Active Requirement Mining of Bounded-Time Temporal Properties of CPS | Chen, Sabato, Kong | 2016 | Mining as parameter synthesis plus falsification (GP active learning). A data-driven proposer, not sound. |
| 1707.03529 | Tunable Reactive Synthesis for Lipschitz-Bounded Systems with TL Specs | Vazquez-Chanlatte, Ghosh, Raman, Sangiovanni-Vincentelli, Seshia | 2017 | A **hierarchy of information levels** for bounded-horizon STL reactive synthesis, solved by CEGIS with optimization and SMT. The closest STL ∀∃ CEGIS. |
| 2012.00319 | Constrained Optimization for Hybrid System Falsification and Conjunctive Synthesis | Sato, Waga, Hasuo | 2020 | Falsification for conjunctive STL with trade-offs. Could serve as a fast pre-SMT counterexample finder. |
| 2403.10554 | Safe Planning through Incremental Decomposition of STL Specifications | Kapoor, Kang, Meira-Goes | 2024 | *Temporal* decomposition into reach/invariance pieces. Not spatial or port-based. |

- **1603.00814.** Formulates mining of bounded-time STL requirements as parameter synthesis plus falsification over robustness, and introduces GP-ACB active learning with a regret bound. *Verdict:* a heuristic proposer of STL parameters from simulations. It has no soundness and needs a system model or simulator.
- **1707.03529.** Defines a hierarchy of finite-horizon STL reactive control problems that differ in how much the system knows about the adversary's moves. Controllers come from a CEGIS loop that mixes optimization and SMT and builds a strategy tree; each counterexample prunes controllers maximally. *Verdict:* **[analysis]** their information hierarchy is the single-controller version of our quantifier prefix ordering (`complete.tex` step 3), and their counterexample-pruning loop is a working STL ∀∃ CEGIS. It needs Lipschitz dynamics and has one controller.
- **2012.00319.** Combines CMA-ES falsification with multiple-constraint ranking to find inputs satisfying conjunctions of conflicting STL requirements. *Verdict:* cheap, unsound search for violating traces, a possible pre-filter before SMT in the loop.
- **2403.10554.** Encodes STL as reachability and invariance constraints scheduled sequentially at runtime to plan more efficiently. *Verdict:* decomposes over time, not over components. It is not our problem.
- **2312.16336.** Proves NP-completeness of learning LTL formulas from positive and negative examples for the full logic and nearly all fragments. *Verdict:* learning separating formulas from counterexamples is intrinsically hard. An LLM proposer can do no better in the worst case, only heuristically.

### Topic 6: Distributed synthesis, Henkin dependencies, and the limits of what agents can find

| arXiv | Title | Authors | Year | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1506.03883 | Hierarchical Information and the Synthesis of Distributed Strategies | Berwanger, Mathew, van den Bogaard | 2015 | Decidable when players are **totally ordered by information**. This is the game-theoretic name for our nested ordering. |
| 2205.12085 | Information Flow Guided Synthesis | Finkbeiner, Metzger, Moses | 2022 | Necessary *information-flow* assumptions (hyperproperties) where no necessary behavioural assumption exists. Explains why forwarding must be imposed. |
| 2407.12298 | Information Flow Guided Synthesis with Unbounded Communication | Finkbeiner, Metzger, Moses | 2024 | Extends the above to unbounded information, using standard synthesis for components (FlowSy). |
| 2105.09221 | Program Synthesis as Dependency Quantified Formula Modulo Theory | Golia, Roy, Meel | 2021 | Grammar-free synthesis reduced to **DQF(T)**, i.e. Henkin quantifiers modulo a theory. Exactly the logical form of (K) with port locality. |
| 2301.10556 | Synthesis with Explicit Dependencies (Manthan3) | Golia, Roy, Meel | 2023 | A **data-driven learn–verify–repair** Henkin synthesizer. The template for a CEGIS proposer under information constraints (Boolean only). |
| 2412.16226 | Quantified LRA/NRA Satisfiability via Template-based Skolemization | Chatterjee, Goharshady, Karrabi, et al. | 2024 | Synthesizes linear Skolem functions in place of QE: sound, semi-complete, subexponential time. A symbolic alternative to our QE. |
| 1111.3106 | Practical Distributed Control Synthesis | Peled, Schewe | 2011 | "Trivial or undecidable" dichotomy; a middle way with scarce communication. Frames forwarding as minimal communication. |
| 1803.09570 | Encodings of Bounded Synthesis | Faymonville, Finkbeiner, Rabe, Tentrup | 2018 | Bounded synthesis as SAT/QBF/DQBF. QBF dominates DQBF in practice. Evidence that linearizing the quantifier order pays. |
| 1804.05507 | What's Hard about Boolean Functional Synthesis | Akshay, Chakraborty, Goel, Kulal, Shah | 2018 | Unless complexity conjectures fail, Skolem functions must be exponential in the worst case. Agents cannot beat this. |

- **1506.03883.** Distributed synthesis for imperfect-information games is undecidable unless information is restricted. The classic decidable case has a total order in which each player knows everything the following players know. The paper extends decidability to hierarchies that change along the play or have transient non-hierarchical phases. *Verdict:* **[analysis]** our (Cap)/(E)/(Info) forwarding conditions *create* such a hierarchy, and the paper explains why the procedure is complete there and only a refutation test elsewhere. A first source for justifying the scope boundary.
- **2205.12085.** Argues that necessary *behavioural* assumptions often do not exist because many behaviours realize a spec. It introduces *information-flow assumptions* (hyperproperties stating that some information must reach a component), derives them automatically from temporal specs, and synthesizes components from them (BoSyHyper). *Verdict:* **directly explains our forwarding step.** Copying `r` onto `m` is an information-flow obligation that no behavioural CEGIS learner would discover from (V1) counterexamples alone, so the agentic loop should impose it symbolically.
- **2407.12298.** Handles the case where the required information is unbounded (streams), derives information-flow assumptions from temporal safety specs, and builds components with ordinary trace-property synthesis (FlowSy), outperforming earlier distributed synthesis. *Verdict:* the same lesson as 2205.12085, for H>0 and streams.
- **2105.09221.** Shows that synthesis without a grammar ("T-constrained synthesis") reduces to finding witnesses of a dependency-quantified formula modulo theory T, DQF(T). For bit-vectors this becomes DQBF, and DQBF solvers then match domain-specific synthesizers. *Verdict:* **[analysis]** "a causal strategy for each component that reads only its own inputs" is a DQF(LRA) problem: each output timed variable has a Henkin dependency set equal to the timed inputs of its component up to that time. The paper names our exact problem class. The LRA case is not solved there.
- **2301.10556.** Manthan3 learns candidate Henkin functions from data (samples), verifies them, and repairs them from counterexamples. It solves 26 DQBF-competition instances no other tool solved. *Verdict:* **the closest algorithmic template for the agentic loop.** The proposer learns per-output functions restricted to their dependency sets, and the verifier returns counterexamples that drive local repair. The open step is moving it from Boolean to LRA and replacing the learner with an LLM.
- **2412.16226.** Avoids QE for quantified LRA/NRA by synthesizing linear or polynomial Skolem functions from templates, using Positivstellensätze. The method is sound, semi-complete, subexponential in time and polynomial in space, and beats SMT solvers on benchmarks. *Verdict:* **a symbolic proposer that targets exactly the cost problem of our QE route.** A template-Skolem search per output, constrained to the component's inputs, is the "symbolic search" alternative to LLM proposers. It matches our semi-decision remark.
- **1111.3106.** Distributed control is either trivial (full synchronization) or undecidable (no communication). The paper proposes scarce communication that favours local decisions. *Verdict:* forwarding is a fixed, minimal communication pattern of this kind.
- **1803.09570.** Compares SAT, QBF and DQBF encodings of bounded LTL synthesis and finds that QBF clearly dominates both. *Verdict:* **[analysis]** supports our choice of one linear quantifier order made sound by forwarding, over a native Henkin (DQBF-style) encoding.
- **1804.05507.** Proves that, under standard conjectures, Boolean functional synthesis requires exponential-size Skolem functions in the worst case. It gives a two-phase algorithm that is efficient on most benchmarks, with a sufficient condition for when the first phase is exact. *Verdict:* worst-case blow-up of strategy size is intrinsic. LLM proposers can only win on instances that have small strategies.

## (b) What exists and what is missing, per topic

**1. Contract and AG synthesis.** *Exists:* sound synthesis of an implementation (Skolem functions) from **one** component's AG contract over LRA via ∀∃ validity (Katis et al., JSyn/AE-VAL). CEGIS for several jointly constrained programs (Relish). Learning of several modular contracts adequate for a client (DUALIS). Contract negotiation loops in control that are model-based. A theory of when CEGIS terminates (Jha–Seshia). *Missing:* no paper takes a **global** AG contract plus an architecture and runs counterexample-guided search for **several port-local component contracts** without component models. The closest systems either have one unknown (JSyn), have the client program as an extra input (DUALIS), or have no temporal I/O semantics and no receptiveness (Relish; also Albarghouthi et al. in `lit/existing-work.md`). None uses the quotient or projection to constrain or complete proposals.

**2. Learning-based AG.** *Exists:* L\*/L#-style learning of the assumption for one given component against one given property, now extended to infinite state by abstraction. ICE and Horn-ICE learners whose sample model already covers multi-premise implications. ICE-DT with numerical attributes. *Missing:* all of it assumes component models, which we do not have, and one unknown per learning problem. Horn-ICE has the right *sample shape* for (V1) counterexamples (n body unknowns, no head). Nobody combines it with the **positive, ∀∃ obligation** that (R)/(K) imposes: a learner that over-shrinks `S_i` violates receptiveness. **[analysis]** An agentic delegation learner therefore faces two-sided feedback. (V1) counterexamples say "some `S_i` must shrink" and need blame assignment. (R)/(K) counterexamples say "`S_i` must grow at this input history". Learning the strategies instead of the sets removes the second kind, because a strategy graph is receptive and causal by construction.

**3. LLMs plus solvers.** *Exists:* a sound calculus for LLM proposals checked by a verifier (Lemur). Many working LLM-plus-SMT CEGIS loops for single unknowns (loop invariants, plans, barrier certificates). Evidence that LLMs should guide a symbolic enumerator rather than replace it (Li–Parsert–Polgreen). Evidence on how to present counterexamples to LLMs (Liu–Sala–Reps–Murali). NL→LTL/STL translation. *Missing:* (i) **no LLM work on assume–guarantee contracts as such**: searches for LLM plus AG contract or contract decomposition returned only smart contracts, legal contracts and the authoring of single specs. (ii) DafnyCOMP shows LLMs specifically *fail* at cross-component specification. (iii) No LLM work on Henkin or DQF synthesis, or on quantified LRA. **[analysis]** The cross-component choice (what goes on the interface, which is our forwarding) should therefore stay symbolic, and LLMs are best placed as proposers of per-component *local* rules (output = piecewise-affine function of own inputs), each checked by one quantifier-free SMT query.

**4. Multi-agent and agentic frameworks.** *Exists:* agent orchestration with fixed roles (generator, refiner, debugger), and "agents propose, solvers verify" with top-down specification inference from callers and compositional checking against callee stubs (Agentic Model Checking). Dependency-aware revisiting of upstream specs (SpecAgent). Obligation-local failure reports (Schwarz). Contracts used to supervise agents (ContrAgent). *Missing:* nothing found where **each agent owns a component and sees only its ports**, so that the information constraint of the problem is mirrored by the agents' views. Nothing found where agents *negotiate* interface obligations and a solver adjudicates. The LLM-negotiation literature found is economic bargaining with no formal verifier, and is excluded.

**5. STL.** *Exists:* data-driven STL mining (requirement mining by falsification), bounded-horizon STL reactive synthesis with an explicit information hierarchy solved by CEGIS (Vazquez-Chanlatte et al.), falsification for conjunctive specs, and temporal decomposition. Spatial STL decomposition (Charitidou, Marchesini, Kanno; see `lit/existing-work.md`) is optimization- or check-based with fixed templates. *Missing:* no counterexample-guided **decomposition** of an STL/PolySTL contract over an I/O architecture; no STL work with agents proposing local specifications.

**6. Distributed synthesis and limits.** *Exists:* undecidability without information hierarchy (Pnueli–Rosner, Finkbeiner–Schewe in `lit/existing-work.md`; Bloem et al. for AG synthesis under partial information). Decidability *with* hierarchical information (Berwanger et al.). Information-flow assumptions as the necessary interface (Finkbeiner–Metzger–Moses). Synthesis as DQF(T) and data-driven Henkin synthesis (Golia–Roy–Meel). Template Skolemization for LRA with subexponential cost (Chatterjee et al.). Exponential worst-case Skolem size (Akshay et al.). *What this means for agents:* **[analysis]**
- On a nested ordering (a hierarchical-information case), a proposed strategy family can be checked by a single quantifier-free SMT query. A correct one exists iff Φ is true. Agents can then replace QE for *finding* the seed but not for *refuting*.
- To refute without QE, an agent would have to propose an environment counter-strategy, and checking that is again quantifier-free. Whether such counter-strategies are small is not addressed in the literature found.
- On forks, a true linearized Φ proves nothing (`complete.tex` §9), and existence is a genuine Henkin/DQF(LRA) question. Agents can still find a valid delegation when one exists (semi-decision), but no agent can be expected to certify nonexistence in general, by the Pnueli–Rosner-type results.
- Strategy size can be exponential (Akshay et al.), and example-based formula learning is NP-hard (Mascle et al.), so agents can only help on instances whose delegations are small.

## (c) Papers most worth reading in full for designing the agentic loop

1. **Golia, Roy, Meel, *Synthesis with Explicit Dependencies* (2301.10556), with *Program Synthesis as DQF(T)* (2105.09221).** They name our problem class: per-output functions restricted to Henkin dependency sets. Manthan3's learn → verify → repair loop is the closest algorithmic template; it is Boolean only, and lifting it to LRA with LLM or template learners is our step.
2. **Chatterjee et al., *Template-based Skolemization for quantified LRA/NRA* (2412.16226).** This is the symbolic-search proposer that attacks the same QE cost, with soundness, semi-completeness and subexponential bounds. It is the baseline any LLM proposer must beat, and possibly the inner engine that an LLM seeds with templates.
3. **Katis et al., *Synthesis from AG Contracts using Skolemized Proofs* (1610.05867), and *Validity-Guided Synthesis* (1709.04986).** Working ∀∃-over-LRA machinery for AG contracts (realizability and Skolem extraction), usable as the (R)/(K) verifier of a proposed contract. The PDR-style refinement is an alternative to full QE per component.
4. **D'Souza et al., *Horn-ICE Learning* (1712.09418), with Uppar et al., *Verification Modulo Tested Library Contracts* (2604.15533).** Horn-ICE gives the sample model for (V1) counterexamples with several unknowns and the blame problem. DUALIS shows a working multi-contract CEGIS with ICE/CHC learners. Read both together to design how one (V1) counterexample is turned into constraints on several components.
5. **Finkbeiner, Metzger, Moses, *Information Flow Guided Synthesis* (2205.12085).** The theoretical reason that interface obligations such as forwarding are information-flow hyperproperties and cannot be learned from behavioural counterexamples. This decides which parts of the loop must stay symbolic.
6. **Sun, Liu, Kroening, Xue, *Agentic Model Checking* (2605.21434).** The closest system architecture, "agents propose, solvers verify": a restricted DSL that compiles deterministically into solver primitives, top-down obligations from caller context, per-unit checking with stubs, and a counterexample-validation pipeline. Read it for the engineering of the loop.
7. **Wu, Barrett, Narodytska, *Lemur* (2310.04870).** How to state and prove soundness of an LLM-in-the-loop procedure as a transition calculus. We will need the same kind of statement: every accepted delegation satisfies (V1), (V2), (R), (K) regardless of what the LLM proposed.
8. **Liu, Sala, Reps, Murali, *Counterexample Guided Learning in the Large using Reasoning Agents* (2606.11521), with Li, Parsert, Polgreen (2403.03997).** Empirical guidance on presenting counterexamples to LLMs (clustering, regularization) and on putting LLMs inside a symbolic search instead of using them alone.

(Background, read as needed: Jha–Seshia 1505.03953/1407.5397 for termination of CEGIS over infinite spaces; Berwanger et al. 1506.03883 for the hierarchical-information scope boundary; Xu et al. 2509.23061 for the measured failure of LLMs on compositional specs.)

## (d) What, as far as this search shows, nobody has done

Each item is a negative finding from the ~30 queries above plus the earlier survey. Absence from arXiv search results is not proof of absence.

1. **No LLM or agent proposer for assume–guarantee contract decomposition.** Nothing found uses LLMs (or multi-agent LLM systems) to propose component contracts whose composition must refine a global AG contract, in any logic. LLM-plus-contract results concern smart and legal contracts, single-function pre/post contracts, or contracts that *supervise* agents.
2. **No counterexample-guided synthesis of several port-local contracts from a global contract with no component models.** JSyn synthesizes one component from its own contract. DUALIS learns several contracts but needs the client program. Relish has several unknowns but no temporal I/O semantics. Multi-abduction (Albarghouthi et al.) has no time, no receptiveness and no loop with an external proposer.
3. **No use of the contract calculus (quotient, projection, composition) as a constraint on proposals.** No paper found uses the quotient to restrict, complete or repair what a learner or LLM proposes. In the literature the quotient appears only as a closed-form operator with one unknown.
4. **No learner handling both kinds of feedback that delegation produces.** That is, negative or implication samples with several unknowns from (V1) *and* ∀∃ receptiveness or causality samples from (R)/(K). Horn-ICE covers the first, JSyn/AE-VAL checks the second, and no work combines them.
5. **No Henkin/DQF synthesis over linear real arithmetic with LLM or data-driven proposers.** DQF(T) is posed in general, but the solvers are Boolean (DQBF, Manthan3). Template Skolemization handles quantified LRA with linear prefixes, not dependency sets.
6. **No STL or PolySTL contract decomposition by search or agents.** STL decomposition is template-plus-optimization (Charitidou, Marchesini), check-only (Kanno), or temporal (Kapoor). STL CEGIS exists for one controller with dynamics (Vazquez-Chanlatte et al.).
7. **No multi-agent framework in which agents own components and see only their ports.** No framework found has agents negotiate interface obligations under an SMT adjudicator. Agent roles in existing frameworks are functional (generate, critique, repair), not architectural.

**[analysis] The resulting opening, in one sentence.** Agents propose per-component causal strategies (piecewise-affine rules over each component's own inputs) on top of the symbolic forwarding step. Each proposal is checked by a single quantifier-free SMT query, `A_P ∧ ⋀(y = f_i(in_i)) ∧ ¬G_P` unsat, whose models are the counterexamples. The quotient and projection sweep is kept, or approximated by agent-proposed enlargements, each of which needs only a (V1) unsat check because (R) and (K) survive enlargement. QE would then be needed only to refute and to certify maximality. According to the search above, no published work has done this.
