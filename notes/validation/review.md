## Findings

### Blocking

**1. (blocking) The speed claim does not survive a one-line change to the monolithic check.** Section 4, Break-even, says delegation is cheaper "from the first revision of the first query" because c_M >= 600 s at H=8. With the same path enumeration, the same solver object, the same branch checks and the same fence queries, and only the goal query posed last-tick-first (a sound strengthening any BMC engineer would try after one timeout), the u-free monolithic check of R4+M2 accepts in 12.26-12.59 s at H=8, against 12.75 + 0.46 = 13.21 s for step 2 plus the planner check on the same machine. It also accepts at H=16 (17.95 s vs 19.93 s) and H=32 (32.41 s vs 31.36 s). With these numbers the break-even inequality gives R Q > c_2/(c_M - c_1) = 12.75/(12.26 - 0.46) = 1.08, so at R = Q = 1 delegation is slower, and it is cheaper only from the second fully verified revision or query. For the paper's own revision sequence R1, R2, R3, R4 at H=8 the monolithic route costs 0.04 + 0.06 + 0.03 + 12.26 = 12.4 s (wrong revisions are refuted in milliseconds, item 5), and delegation costs 12.75 s plus four planner checks, so delegation is slower for the scenario the paper runs. Evidence: `quiet_timing.jsonl`, `alt_path.py`, log 03:01-03:03. Required change: remove "from the first revision of the first query", "c_M >= 600 s" as the headline, and the cumulative-cost figure built on it (Fig. 2 right, and the 0.032 break-even). Replace with the measured c_M of the best monolithic encoding tried (12.3 s at H=8, 18.0 s at H=16, 32.4 s at H=32) and state plainly: against this encoding, delegation and monolithic checking cost the same for one verified revision; delegation is cheaper once two or more correct revisions or queries are verified against one certified controller configuration (break-even R Q about 1.1); it is slower when the revisions that fail are cheap to refute. If the authors keep the 600 s figure, it must be labelled as "the monolithic check with the goal conjunction posed as one query on Z3's incremental solver", not as c_M.

**2. (blocking) The write-up does not explain why the monolithic check times out, and the explanation is an artifact of the encoding, not of monolithic verification.** Mechanism, found by profiling (`mono_profile.py`, `mono_profile2.py`, `hard_query.py`): at H=4 the paper's check completes 5 952 solver calls (all branch checks and fence queries of 112 paths) in 2.3 s of solver time; the remaining time is one call in flight, the goal query `And_t (y_t-g)^2 > 9` of one path, nine quadratic atoms in (s, h, g, p0). It is not path explosion (branch checks total 0.8 s), not symbolic length classes (the classes are decided by linear branch checks), and not the fence atoms over u (linear, all decided in milliseconds). Two facts combine. (i) In step 2 the reach query compares each target with w_2, and on every path the last target is syntactically w_2, so the query is decided by simplification; in the monolithic check the goal is the input g, and the last target is clip(g), so deciding it needs nonlinear reasoning over u (|clip(g) - g| <= 0.5·sqrt 2 < 3 under A). (ii) The query is posed on the shared path solver after push, i.e. in Z3's incremental SMT core, whose nonlinear arithmetic is incomplete and diverges here. The same extracted query is unsat in 0.00 s on a fresh default solver, 0.00 s with qfnra-nlsat, 0.00 s for the last-tick atom alone, and 5.66 s on a fresh push/pop solver; on the shared path solver it had not returned after 10 s, and in the paper's run after 600 s. The fresh-solver variant without any domain knowledge also finishes (accept at H=4 in 274 s under load). Required change: state this mechanism in Section 4 (two sentences), and drop "so the comparison is like for like": the machinery is shared but the property queries are not of the same kind, and the timeout belongs to one query of one encoding.

**3. (blocking, presentation of the same issue) Table 1 and Fig. 2 compare a failed certification with a timeout at H=4.** At H=4 step 2 does not certify (reach sat), so delegation gives no verdict at all, while R4+M2 is in fact correct at H=4 (my exhaustive `last` check accepts in 9.4 s; 100 000 concrete runs show no violation; at H=3 37 % of runs violate G). The row "step 2 total" at H=2 and H=4 and the plotted "step 2, once per horizon" points there are times of checks that end in "no controller guarantee". Required change: mark the H=2 and H=4 step-2 entries as "not certified (reach fails)" in Table 1 and Fig. 2, and state in E3 that at H=4 the monolithic route can prove the composition correct where delegation cannot (an instance of Delta_V/Delta_T conservatism: some mission in V but none that R4 emits fails to finish in 4 ticks).

**3a. (blocking) The 03:10 tex contradicts itself.** Section 5 ("The speed claim is conditional ... it is not faster for a single accepted check, wrong revisions are cheap to refute on both routes, and below the smallest certified horizon it gives no verdict") now states the findings of items 1-3, while Table 1, E3, Fig. 2 and the Break-even paragraph still say c_M >= 600 s, break-even R Q > 0.032 and "from the first revision of the first query". Required change: rerun the full `validate.py` with the new `order='fair'`, and replace the Table 1 row, E3, Fig. 2 and the Break-even numbers with the fair-order numbers (the 03:11 quick run gives c_M = 13.17 s, c_2 = 15.48 s, c_1 = 0.38 s at H=8, break-even R Q > 1.21, R1-R4 sequence 13.33 s monolithic against 15.87 s delegated); keep the original-order timeout only as a documented artefact.

### Major

**4. (major) The cost model charges every monolithic check c_M.** In `R Q c_M`, a monolithic check that refutes a planner costs milliseconds (R1 0.04 s, R2 0.06 s, R3 0.03 s at H=8 with u free, in the paper's own encoding; Table 1 already shows 0.06 s at H=2), while delegation still pays c_2 once. The inequality needs c_M split into accept and reject costs, or R replaced by the number of revisions that must be fully proved. Required change: write the monolithic cost as R_acc c_M^acc + R_rej c_M^rej and say that for the R1-R4 sequence only R4 costs c_M^acc.

**5. (major) The only measured advantage that survives is amortisation, and it should be the headline.** With c_M of item 1, delegation wins exactly when one certified controller configuration serves several fully verified planners or queries (e.g. Q queries with different A_1/G on the same horizon and P, or several correct planner revisions). Required change: reword the abstract-level claim to "delegation amortises the controller check; it is cheaper than a monolithic BMC of planner and controller once two or more planner revisions or queries are verified against one certified controller configuration; for a single verification the two cost the same in our instance".

**6. (major) The lower-bound treatment is honest about the timeout, but the conclusion drawn from it is not.** "the inequality holds once RQ > 0.032 (an upper bound, as c_M is a lower bound)" is correct arithmetic, but Fig. 2 (right) plots R·600 s as if it were a cost and shows separation "at once". A lower bound from a timed-out encoding is evidence about that encoding only. Required change: either drop Fig. 2 right or redraw it with the measured c_M of the alternative encoding, with the 600 s curve labelled as a timeout of one encoding.

**7. (major) Statement of when delegation is slower: present but incomplete.** The text covers fixed inputs (factor 410), R = Q = 1 when c_M <= c_2, sigma close to Q, outside Phi, and incremental monolithic checks. It misses (a) the case that actually occurs here, c_M about equal to c_2 for one verified revision (item 1); (b) cheap refutation of wrong revisions (item 4); (c) horizons below the smallest certified one, where delegation has no verdict and monolithic checking does (item 3). Required change: add these three.

**8. (major) "401 fixed inputs" and "factor of 410" use two different ratios in one sentence.** 410 = (c_2 + c_1)/0.0472 s; 401 = c_2/0.0472 s (results.json: c_2 = 18.931, c_1 = 0.405, mean fixed-u time 0.04720). Required change: use one consistent ratio and say which delegated cost it assumes (with or without the u-free planner check). Also say that the fixed-input mean at H=2 averages accepts and rejects (results.json `statuses`).

## Verdict on the speed claim

As worded ("delegation plus separate discharge is faster than monolithic bounded model checking of planner plus controller", "from the first revision of the first query", c_M >= 600 s), the claim does not survive. It survives against the paper's own monolithic encoding and against every single-formula encoding I tried (16 variants, all timeouts or unknown at 600 s). It does not survive against a path-enumeration monolithic check that differs from the paper's only in posing the goal query last-tick-first: at H=8 that check accepts in 12.3 s against 13.2 s for step 2 plus the planner check, at H=16 18.0 s against 19.9 s, at H=32 32.4 s against 31.4 s, and at H=4 it proves the composition correct where delegation has no verdict. What survives is amortisation: delegation is cheaper once two or more fully verified planner revisions or queries share one certified controller configuration.
